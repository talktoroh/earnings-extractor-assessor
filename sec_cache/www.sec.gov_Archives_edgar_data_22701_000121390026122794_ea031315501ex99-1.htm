SUNATION ENERGY ANNOUNCES THIRD QUARTER 2026 RESULTS; REPORTS SEQUENTIAL REVENUE AND GROSS MARGIN IMPROVEMENT AND PROVIDES SUNIVA MERGER UPDATE

 

Third-quarter revenue of $11.2 million increased from $8.2 million in the second quarter; year-over-year results continued to reflect lower residential demand following the expiration of the Section 25D federal residential tax credit.

 

Commercial revenue increased 20% year-over-year to $2.7 million; SG&A expense declined 37% year-over-year to $4.3 million.

 

Results included $9.8 million of non-cash goodwill and intangible asset impairment charges.

 

SUNation continues to prioritize liquidity, cost management, and execution of its proposed merger with Suniva.

 

RONKONKOMA, N.Y., NOVEMBER 20, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- SUNation Energy, Inc. (Nasdaq: SUNE) (“SUNation” or the “Company”), a leading provider of residential and commercial solar energy systems, battery storage solutions and comprehensive energy services, today announced financial results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026 (“Q3 2026”).

 

Third-quarter revenue increased sequentially to $11.2 million from $8.2 million, and gross margin improved to 30.8% from 26.1%, reflecting higher revenue across the Company’s New York and Hawaii operations. Year-over-year performance continued to reflect downward pressure from the residential solar market’s adjustment to the expiration of the Section 25D federal residential tax credit at the end of 2025 under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (“OBBBA”). Consolidated revenue declined 41% year over year, with lower residential revenue partially offset by growth in commercial revenue.

 

SUNation Reduces Costs and Advances Suniva Merger

 

The Company continued to manage its cost structure, reducing selling, general and administrative (“SG&A”) expense by 37% year over year. Results also included $9.8 million of non-cash goodwill and intangible asset impairment charges following an assessment of asset recoverability in light of lower forecast revenue and operating results. Including these charges, SUNation reported an operating loss of $11.7 million and a net loss of $11.8 million.

 

SUNation also continued to advance its proposed reverse merger with Suniva, Inc. (“Suniva”). SUNation currently anticipates closing in the first quarter of 2027, subject to satisfaction or waiver of applicable closing conditions and receipt of required approvals and regulatory clearances.

 

 

 

 

Q3 2026 HIGHLIGHTS

 

–

Revenue and gross margin improved sequentially

 

Revenue increased to $11.2 million from $8.2 million in Q2 2026, and gross margin improved to 30.8% from 26.1%. Revenue declined 41% year over year, primarily reflecting lower residential installation activity.

 

–

Commercial revenue grew 20% year over year

 

Commercial revenue reached $2.66 million, compared with $2.23 million in Q3 2025, primarily driven by growth in New York.

 

–

SG&A expense declined 37% year over year

 

SG&A expense decreased to $4.33 million from $6.90 million, reflecting lower selling and marketing expenses and personnel cost reductions.

 

–

Net loss reflected non-cash impairment charges

 

Net loss was $11.82 million, compared with $0.39 million in Q3 2025. Results included $9.82 million of non-cash goodwill and intangible asset impairment charges reflecting lower forecast revenue and operating results.

 

–

Suniva merger process advanced

 

The companies amended their merger agreement during the quarter to address financing, stockholder approvals and closing requirements. Closing is currently anticipated in Q1 2027, subject to applicable conditions and approvals.

 

Commercial Growth Supports Revenue Diversification

 

The quarter’s sequential revenue and gross-margin improvement occurred within a residential market that continued to operate below prior-year activity levels. Residential revenue declined 52% year over year, while commercial revenue increased 20%. Commercial and service revenue together represented approximately 33% of consolidated revenue, compared with approximately 18% in Q3 2025.

 

The prior-year quarter also benefited from residential demand ahead of the year-end 2025 tax credit expiration, contributing to the year-over-year comparison. Management continues to emphasize commercial projects, battery storage, service and adjacent energy offerings as part of its efforts to diversify revenue.

 

Storage Opportunities Support Adaptation to Residential Market Changes

 

The expiration of the Section 25D federal residential tax credit at year-end 2025 under OBBBA continued to affect residential demand, installation volumes and financing economics during Q3 2026, following elevated activity ahead of the expiration. Lower installation volumes also increased fixed labor and overhead costs as a percentage of revenue.

 

Battery storage remains an area of focus. Hawaii’s Bring Your Own Device Plus program provides incentives for qualifying customers to add storage to existing or new rooftop solar systems. Hawaii Energy Connection’s battery attachment rates increased 13% year over year during the quarter.

Updated Asset Values and Liquidity Priorities

 

The Company’s impairment assessment reflected a material reduction in forecast revenue and operating results. Goodwill impairment charges related to the SUNation Residential and SUNation Commercial reporting units, while intangible asset impairment charges related to tradenames and trademarks across the New York and Hawaii businesses.

 

SUNation continues to prioritize cash preservation, disciplined working capital management and additional financing to support operations and maintain financial flexibility. The Company ended the quarter with $2.79 million in cash and cash equivalents and requires additional capital to meet its obligations and operating needs.

 

MANAGEMENT COMMENTARY

 

“Third-quarter revenue and gross margin improved from the second quarter as activity increased across our New York and Hawaii businesses,” said Scott Maskin, Chief Executive Officer of SUNation. “Commercial revenue grew 20% year over year, and commercial and service work represented a larger share of our business. Residential demand continues to reflect the change in customer economics following the expiration of the federal residential tax credit, and our teams remain focused on matching our offerings and operating structure to the market we are serving.”

 

Mr. Maskin continued, “Our priorities remain disciplined execution, customer service, cost management and liquidity. We are pursuing commercial projects, storage installations, service work, roofing and adjacent energy opportunities while managing expenses closely. We also continued to advance the Suniva merger process during the quarter, including an amendment to the merger agreement addressing financing and closing-related matters.”

 

James Brennan, SUNation’s Chief Financial Officer and Chief Operations Officer, said, “Higher sequential revenue supported gross-margin improvement to 30.8%, while year-over-year SG&A expense declined 37% following reductions in selling, marketing and personnel costs. Our reported results also included $9.8 million of non-cash impairment charges reflecting the effect of lower forecast revenue and operating results on the carrying value of goodwill and intangible assets.”

 

Mr. Brennan added, “Cash preservation, working capital management and access to additional capital remain essential. We ended September with $2.8 million of cash and cash equivalents, and our working capital position continues to require close management. We are evaluating financing sources and managing our obligations while supporting customer commitments and the proposed transaction.”

Q3 2026 FINANCIAL AND OPERATIONAL RESULTS

 

FINANCIAL RESULTS

 

  –

Revenue

 

Consolidated revenue was $11.20 million, compared with $18.99 million in Q3 2025, a decrease of 41%. Residential contract revenue declined 52%, partially offset by 20% growth in commercial revenue.

 

  –

Gross profit and margin

 

Gross profit was $3.45 million, compared with $7.21 million in Q3 2025. Gross margin was 30.8%, compared with 38.0% in the prior-year quarter, reflecting lower revenue and fixed labor and overhead costs that declined more slowly than sales volume. Gross profit and margin improved sequentially from approximately $2.1 million and 26.1%, respectively, in Q2 2026.

 

  –

Operating expenses and cost reductions

 

Total operating expenses were $15.16 million, compared with $7.46 million in Q3 2025, including $9.82 million of non-cash goodwill and intangible asset impairment charges and $0.46 million of Suniva merger transaction costs. SG&A expense declined 37% to $4.33 million from $6.90 million, reflecting lower selling and marketing expenses and personnel cost reductions.

 

  –

Net loss

 

Net loss was $11.82 million, or $1.81 per basic and diluted share, compared with $0.39 million, or $0.12 per basic and diluted share, in Q3 2025. The increase reflected the impairment charges and lower gross profit, partially offset by reduced SG&A expense. Operating loss was $11.71 million, compared with $0.25 million in Q3 2025. Impairment charges comprised $4.92 million of goodwill and $4.90 million of intangible assets. Amortization expense was unchanged year over year at $0.56 million.

For the nine months ended September 30, 2026, revenue was $26.56 million, gross profit was $7.17 million and SG&A expense was $13.87 million, compared with $44.69 million, $16.48 million and $19.38 million, respectively, in the prior-year period. Net loss, including the third-quarter impairment charges, was $19.25 million, compared with $13.50 million in the prior year.

STRATEGIC INITIATIVES AND PROPOSED SUNIVA MERGER

 

SUNation continued to advance its proposed reverse merger with Suniva during Q3 2026. The transaction, announced in June following the Board’s review of strategic alternatives, would result in Suniva becoming a wholly owned subsidiary of SUNation.

 

On September 4, 2026, the parties amended the merger agreement to address financing arrangements, stockholder approvals and closing requirements. The amendment revised SUNation’s closing net cash requirement to negative $2.5 million, subject to adjustment for potential capital raises, and added a proposed increase in authorized common shares from 1.0 billion to 1.5 billion to the matters requiring stockholder approval.

 

The amendment also provides for SUNation to use reasonable best efforts to repay or convert into equity up to approximately $2.61 million of specified related-party loans. Any conversion would be at $2.26 per share and subject to stockholder approval (with any related parties abstaining from the vote). These arrangements remain subject to the merger agreement, as amended.

 

The Company has confidentially submitted a registration statement on Form S-4 containing a proxy statement/prospectus relating to the proposed transaction. Closing remains subject to required stockholder approvals, effectiveness of the registration statement, Nasdaq listing approval and other applicable closing conditions and regulatory clearances. The timing and completion of the transaction remain uncertain.

 

Alongside the merger process, SUNation continued to manage its capital resources. During the first nine months of 2026, the Company completed a private placement generating approximately $2.7 million in gross proceeds for working capital and general corporate purposes, converted $1.2 million of related-party note principal into equity and made approximately $1.2 million of payments against third-party and related-party loans.

 

BUSINESS STRATEGY AND OUTLOOK

 

SUNation’s near-term priorities are to preserve liquidity, manage working capital, align costs with current revenue levels and advance the proposed Suniva merger. Management expects residential demand and installation activity to remain sensitive to customer financing economics and the market’s adjustment to the expiration of the Section 25D federal residential tax credit.

 

The Company remains focused on serving customers in New York and Hawaii while diversifying revenue through commercial projects, battery storage, maintenance and repair, roofing and adjacent energy services. Management will continue to evaluate financing alternatives to support operations and maintain financial flexibility as it pursues these priorities.

Non-GAAP Financial Measures

 

This press release also includes non-GAAP financial measures that differ from financial measures calculated in accordance with United States generally accepted accounting principles (“GAAP”). Adjusted EBITDA is a non-GAAP financial measure provided in this release, and is net loss calculated in accordance with GAAP, adjusted for interest, income taxes, depreciation, amortization, stock compensation, transaction costs, gain on sale of assets, earnout consideration compensation, financing fees, and non-cash fair value remeasurement adjustments as detailed in the reconciliations presented below in this press release.

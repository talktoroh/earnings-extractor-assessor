Eos Energy Enterprises Reports Third Quarter 2026 Revenue of $82.7 Million, Up 171% Year Over Year, with Gross Margin Improving 58 Percentage Points • Delivered record revenue of $82.7 million through deliveries to 10 projects • Gross margin improved 58 percentage points and adjusted EBITDA margin improved 96 percentage points year over year, the eighth consecutive quarter both metrics improved • Received $87 million first advance under the second tranche of the U.S. Department of Energy loan • Announced a project with MN8 Energy to deliver 10 MW/100 MWh of Z3™ zinc-based LDES in West Virginia, serving Google’s data centers • Discharged 6.9 GWh of cumulative energy from Eos technology, up nearly 400 MWh sequentially • Line 2’s performance reached first-pass yield parity with Line 1 within three months of operation, and Thorn Hill moved to 24-hour operations in October • Reaffirms full-year revenue outlook in the lower half of its $300 million - $350 million range Pittsburgh, PA – November 5, 2026 – Eos Energy Enterprises, Inc. (NASDAQ: EOSE) (“Eos” or the “Company”), America’s leading innovator in designing, manufacturing, and providing zinc-based long- duration energy storage (LDES) systems sourced and manufactured in the United States, today announced its financial results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026. Third Quarter Highlights • Revenue totaled $82.7 million, up 171% year over year and 20% sequentially, driven by a 216% increase in cube shipments year over year and 23% growth sequentially. • Gross margin of negative 53%, improving 58 percentage points year over year and 18 percentage points sequentially, driven by lower material costs, increased production volumes, and lower conversion costs. Gross loss was $44.2 million, compared to $33.9 million in the prior year period. • Adjusted gross margin improved to negative 44%, with adjusted gross loss improving $6.1 million sequentially to $36.8 million, excluding stock-based compensation and depreciation. • Net income attributable to shareholders totaled $125.0 million, primarily reflecting non-cash mark- to-market gains on warrants and debt resulting from changes in the Company's stock price. • Adjusted EBITDA margin was negative 77%, improving 96 percentage points year over year and 27 percentage points sequentially. Adjusted EBITDA loss improved to $63.8 million from $71.4 million in the prior quarter, reflecting improved operating leverage on 20% sequential revenue growth. • Total cash, including restricted cash, was $344.1 million as of September 30, 2026. • Backlog totaled $765 million, representing 3.2 GWh, up 19% year over year and down 5% from prior quarter, reflecting backlog conversion into record quarterly revenue of $82.7 million, partially offset by new bookings during the quarter, as of September 30, 2026. "Our third quarter shows operating model improvement. We delivered our highest quarterly revenue, improved gross margin by 18 points sequentially, and brought Line 2 first-pass yield equal to Line 1 four times faster," said Joe Mastrangelo, Chief Executive Officer of Eos. "While the transition to Thorn Hill lowers fourth-quarter capacity, it positions us to reduce cost per cube and improve manufacturing efficiency in future quarters. We remain on track to achieve the lower half of our full-year revenue outlook.” 2026 Revenue Outlook The Company expects full-year 2026 revenue in the lower half of its $300 million to $350 million range. The Company accelerated the consolidation of manufacturing into Thorn Hill, beginning in late October, including the relocation and upgrade of Line 1 with capabilities incorporated into Line 2. This strategic move is expected to temporarily impact production capacity and the timing of certain shipments while accelerating the realization of a lower-cost, single-site manufacturing footprint and associated operating efficiencies in 2027.
Recent Business Highlights Commercial Momentum Eos ended the third quarter with a backlog of $765 million and a commercial opportunity pipeline of $24.9 billion. During the quarter, the Company booked six orders from four new customers and two existing customers. This included a previously announced $100 million purchase order that converted under an existing capacity reservation agreement and, as a result, did not contribute to quarter-over-quarter backlog growth. Demand continued across the commercial and industrial (C&I) and data center markets. Eos announced an agreement with MN8 Energy and Google to deliver 10 MW/100 MWh of Z3™ zinc-based LDES in West Virginia, serving Google’s data centers in the region. Thorn Hill Production Ramp Eos continued to scale production at its Thorn Hill facility during the third quarter, its first full quarter with both battery lines in commercial production. Shipments increased 23% sequentially, driven by higher utilization at Thorn Hill and continued productivity gains at Turtle Creek. Line 2 contributed 17% of total battery output, up from approximately 1% in the second quarter, while operating on a partial shift schedule, and delivered approximately 20% higher battery output per shift than Line 1. Line 2 also reached first-pass yield parity with Line 1 within three months, demonstrating the design enhancements and manufacturing learnings built into the second line. Consolidation of Manufacturing Operations into Thorn Hill Eos began consolidating manufacturing operations into Thorn Hill. Line 1 disassembly at Turtle Creek began in late October, and the full line is expected to resume production near the end of the first quarter of 2027 with upgraded capabilities based on line 2 enhancements. Upon completion, Thorn Hill is expected to have approximately 4 GWh of annual nameplate capacity across two production lines. About Eos Energy Enterprises Eos is accelerating the shift to American energy independence with positively ingenious solutions that transform how the world stores power. The Company’s BESS features the innovative Znyth™ technology, a proven chemistry with readily available non-precious earth components, that is the pre-eminent safe, non- flammable, secure, stable, and scalable alternative to conventional technology. The Company’s BESS is ideal for utility-scale, microgrid, commercial, and industrial long-duration energy storage applications (i.e., 4 to 16+ hours) and provides customers with significant operational flexibility to effectively address current and future increased grid demand and complexity. Key Metrics Backlog. Our backlog represents the amount of revenue that we expect to realize from existing agreements with our customers for the sale of our battery energy storage systems and performance of services. The backlog is calculated by adding new orders in the current fiscal period to the backlog as of the end of the prior fiscal period and then subtracting the shipments in the current fiscal period. If the amount of an order is modified or cancelled, we adjust orders in the current period and our backlog accordingly, but do not retroactively adjust previously published backlogs. There is no comparable U.S. GAAP financial measure to backlog. We believe that the backlog is a useful indicator regarding the future revenue of our Company. Pipeline. Our pipeline represents projects for which we have submitted technical proposals or non-binding quotes plus letters of intent (“LOI”) or firm commitments from customers. Pipeline does not include lead generation projects. Booked Orders. Booked orders are orders where we have legally binding agreements with a Purchase Order (“PO”) or Master Supply Agreement (“MSA”) executed by both parties.
